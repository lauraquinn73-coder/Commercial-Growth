How to Measure Enquiry-to-Sale Conversion Without a Complicated CRM

You do not need an enterprise CRM to understand whether enquiries are becoming customers. For many small service businesses, a simple and consistently defined funnel is more useful than a sophisticated system nobody maintains.

By Laura Quinn · Commercial Growth · 15 September 2026

Start with one clear definition of an enquiry

Decide what counts. It might be a completed contact form, phone enquiry, direct message, quote request or consultation booking. Avoid mixing casual social interactions with genuine buying intent unless they are handled as sales opportunities. Consistency matters more than complexity.

Define the stages between enquiry and sale

A useful basic funnel might be: Enquiry → Contacted → Qualified → Proposal/Booking Offered → Sale. Your business may need fewer or more stages, but every stage should represent a real commercial change rather than an administrative status.

Calculate the conversion rate

For a chosen period, divide the number of sales by the number of enquiries and multiply by 100. If 40 genuine enquiries produce 10 sales, enquiry-to-sale conversion is 25%. Track the same definition each period so the trend is meaningful.

Measure stage-to-stage conversion too

Overall conversion tells you that something is happening; stage conversion helps reveal where. If most enquiries are contacted but very few become qualified, the issue may be targeting or qualification. If many qualified opportunities receive proposals but few buy, look at pricing, offer clarity, objections and follow-up.

Record a reason when an opportunity is lost

Keep the list short enough that people will actually use it: price, no response, timing, competitor, unsuitable fit, internal delay, changed mind, or other. Over time, the pattern is more useful than individual anecdotes.

Track speed as well as outcome

Response time can materially affect conversion in enquiry-led businesses. Measure the gap between the enquiry arriving and the first meaningful response. If strong leads are waiting hours or days, conversion improvements may come from process rather than more marketing.

Use a simple weekly view

A spreadsheet is enough to start. Track the date, source, stage, owner, value if known, outcome and lost reason. Review totals once a week. The objective is not perfect data; it is enough visibility to make better decisions.

What to do next

Once the funnel is defined, you can test changes deliberately. Improve one stage, compare the result with the baseline and keep what works. That is far more useful than changing scripts, ads or offers without knowing where the real constraint sits.
